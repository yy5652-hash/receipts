# Briefing: Obesity and overweight and CO₂ emissions

Obesity is the leading chronic disease affecting 1.1 billion people worldwide and is responsible for all metabolic disorders [1]. The world's largest per capita CO₂ emitters are exclusively Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, and Kuwait, with no other countries close behind [2]. In 1990, 2% of children and adolescents aged 5–19 were living with obesity, equivalent to 31 million young people [1]. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models combine pre-trained parametric memory with non-parametric memory for language generation [3].

The United States has emitted around 400 billion tonnes of CO₂ since 1751, responsible for almost one-quarter of historical emissions [2]. The Amazon rainforest contains an estimated 390 billion individual trees in about 16,000 species [2]. The Japanese Experiment Module (JEM), or Kibō, was announced in 1985 as part of the Freedom space station [4]. The International Space Station orbits Earth at an altitude of about 400 kilometres (250 miles) [4]. Data centre electricity consumption per capita in the United States is projected to grow to over 1 100 kWh by the end of the decade [5]. Bird species richness declines by 32% within 1 km of forest edges in fragmented southern Amazon landscapes [6].
