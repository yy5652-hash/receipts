# Briefing: Burj Khalifa and Training Compute-Optimal Large Language

Log loss correlates well with many downstream NLP tasks [1]. For the compute budget used to train Gopher, an optimal model should be 4 times smaller and trained on 4 times more tokens [2]. Chinchilla achieves a state-of-the-art average accuracy of 67.5% on the GLUE benchmark [2]. The paper presents image synthesis results using diffusion probabilistic models, a class of latent variable models inspired by nonequilibrium thermodynamics [3]. When the diffusion consists of small amounts of Gaussian noise, the sampling chain transitions must be set to conditional Gaussians, enabling a simple neural network parameterization [3]. The central pinnacle pipe of the Burj Khalifa weighs 180 tonnes and is 200 m tall [1]. The best results were obtained by training on a weighted variational bound based on a novel connection between diffusion probabilistic models and denoising score matching with Langevin dynamics [3]. Chinchilla has 70 billion parameters and was trained on 4 times more data than Gopher [2]. Coral colonies in the central Great Barrier Reef hosting Cladocopium goreaui exhibit 40% higher thermal tolerance during marine heatwaves [4]. The implementation of the model is available at https://github.com/hojonathanho/diffusion [3].
