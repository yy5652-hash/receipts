# Briefing: Apollo 11 and Human Genome Project

Apollo 11 was the American spaceflight that definitively achieved the first human landing on the Moon and was the fifth crewed mission of NASA's Apollo program [1]. Neil Armstrong became the first human to walk on the Moon approximately six hours after landing, followed by Buzz Aldrin nineteen minutes later [1]. The Apollo spacecraft consisted of three parts: the command module, the service module, and the Lunar Module [1]. Walter Gilbert, Frederick Sanger, and Paul Berg invented DNA sequencing methods in 1983 [2]. The world now emits over 35 billion tonnes of CO₂ each year [3]. Since 1700, the world has emitted over 1.5 trillion tonnes of CO₂ [3]. The Amazon rainforest covers 5,500,000 km² (2,100,000 sq mi) [4]. The blue whale was abundant in nearly all the Earth's oceans until the end of the 19th century [5]. By 2035, global data centre electricity consumption is projected to reach around 1 200 TWh in the IEA Base Case [4]. The world emitted 6 billion tonnes of CO₂ in 1950 [3].
