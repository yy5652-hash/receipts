# Briefing: how much electricity will AI data centres use?

*Prepared by an AI research assistant for a policy seminar.*

## Where demand stands today

Data centres used around 415 terawatt-hours (TWh) of electricity in 2024, about 1.5% of global electricity consumption [2]. Their consumption has grown by roughly 20% per year over the last five years [2]. The United States accounted for 45% of global data centre electricity use in 2024, followed by Europe at 25% and China at 15% [1]. Inside a modern data centre, servers account for around 60% of electricity demand, while cooling ranges from about 7% in efficient hyperscale sites to over 30% in less efficient enterprise sites [2].

## Where it is heading

The IEA expects data centre electricity consumption to more than double to around 945 TWh by 2030, slightly more than Japan's total electricity consumption today [1]. In the United States, data centres could account for up to 12% of total electricity use by 2030 [1]. Half of the global growth in data centre demand is expected to be met by renewables [1]. Natural gas will supply all of the remaining growth, expanding by 175 TWh [1].

## Why AI changes the picture

General-purpose generative models are far more energy-intensive at inference time than task-specific models built for a single job [3]. A 2024 study in Nature Energy found that a single chatbot query uses ten times the electricity of a web search [4]. Inference can account for up to 90% of a model's lifecycle [5]. Cooling also carries a water cost: a typical data centre uses around 300,000 gallons of water per day [6].
